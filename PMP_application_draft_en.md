### Project Objective

Reduce uncertainty for surgeons before medical interventions by developing patient-specific 3D printed anatomical models based on real patient anatomy. After validating the approach in clinical cases, the initiative evolved into MedicalSim, focused on developing a lower-cost laparoscopic training simulator for medical residents. The simulator was designed to support haptic practice and capture performance metrics such as number of movements, traveled distance and movement speed.

### Role and Responsibilities

As co-founder and project manager, I led the project from concept through validation, MVP delivery and commercial launch. My responsibilities included defining scope, planning the product roadmap, coordinating iterative validation with medical advisors, prioritizing prototypes and backlog items, coordinating software, hardware, design and 3D printing activities, managing budget and risks, preparing product documentation, validating the MVP and planning the commercial launch.

The core team included Augusto Devesa as project manager, George Kassis as designer and 3D printing specialist, three medical advisors and one developer.

### Outcome

The project delivered patient-specific 3D anatomical models for approximately 50 medical cases across oncology, peripheral cardiovascular surgery, gynecology and urology. In applicable cases, the models helped reduce surgical intervention time by approximately one hour.

The laparoscopic simulator completed demo and MVP phases for both hardware and software. The project also delivered operation manuals, a production-ready product and commercial launch. The solution is currently commercialized through MedicalSim. Six units were sold to institutions including the Hospital Italiano de Buenos Aires training center, Hospital Privado in Mar del Plata and the Sociedad Argentina de Ginecologia.
